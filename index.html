SOUND FAMILIAR?

The best group chats are already scrapbooks. They just don't know it yet.

Somewhere in a group chat with your closest friends, there are years of photos nobody can find again. Snapchat throws them away on purpose. Instagram wants them polished for strangers. The group chat buries them under a thousand other messages by next week.

None of it was ever built to become something you'd actually look back on.

THE CONCEPT

Here's the idea.

You create private groups with friends (from your contacts or an invite link). Inside a group, anyone can start a "stream" for the day — a shared, story-style collection of photos that friends add to throughout the day.

Unlike Snapchat, nothing disappears: streams are saved permanently, building up into a shared photo diary over time. Streams are automatically named by date but can be relabeled — "Saturday night hang."

One idea we're considering: you can only see what your friends posted that day once you've posted something yourself — otherwise you'd see it the next day.

HOW IT WORKS

Three things happen. That's it.

01

Start a group with people you actually know

From your contacts, or a link you send yourself. No public profiles, no strangers.

02

Someone opens today's stream

Could be a lazy Tuesday or a Saturday night — whoever's around kicks it off.

03

Everyone adds their day, whenever they want

No pressure to post. Nothing gets deleted. It just becomes part of the record.
